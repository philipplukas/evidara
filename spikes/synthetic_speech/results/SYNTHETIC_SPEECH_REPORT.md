## The three hypotheses

### Hypothesis 1 — temporal variation is low-dimensional

**Supported, with a caveat about the exact dimension.** {"R_1": 0.9995983151120628, "R_2": 0.9998945983756156, "R_3": 0.9999236796512505}; status IDENTIFIABLE. R_1 = 0.99960 already at noise scale 0.1 -- one temporal component carries almost everything. The generative rank is 2, but the second mode holds only 0.030% of the energy because the two damped responses are nearly collinear curves.

On counting the modes: detected counts by noise scale: {0.03: 1, 0.1: 1, 0.3: 1, 1.0: 1}; the true count 2 is recovered at no noise scale(s). Low-dimensionality is easy to confirm; the exact dimension is not, because a near-collinear second mode becomes statistically invisible long before it becomes physically absent.

### Hypothesis 2 — dynamics are identifiable only modulo latent coordinate changes

**Supported.** the two latents differ by 42% and agree to 5.74e-02 once an affine map is allowed. The recoverable object is the equivalence class (Phi, F) / coordinate changes -- Hypothesis 2, measured.

And the invariant part: a change of latent basis conjugates the state matrix, and conjugation preserves the spectrum. So the poles are the right thing to report, and A_u is not -- two estimators disagreeing about A_u may be describing exactly the same dynamics.

### Hypothesis 3 — explicit dynamics help where statistical models struggle

- **In distribution, does the structured model beat a generic temporal model?** → PARTIALLY IDENTIFIABLE. this is the number the framework should NOT be sold on. In distribution the two are close, which is the expected result if the generic model simply learns the same trajectories without the parameterisation.
- **Does the structured model degrade less on an unseen speaker?** → PARTIALLY IDENTIFIABLE. drop for the generic model: 0.236; for the structured model: 0.461. Both are zero-shot -- no adaptation data from the new speaker is given to anyone.
- **ABLATION -- is it the dynamics that carry the structured model?** → NO EFFECT (negative result). randomising the dynamics costs at most -0.006 accuracy, and removing anticipation costs 0.230. On this task the discriminative information is in WHERE the trajectory is heading, not in HOW it gets there: a random stable system still settles on the right theta, so the class templates stay separable. Across noise levels the dynamics gap is {0.3: -0.009, 1.0: 0.006}, so this is not an artefact of an easy SNR. The honest reading is that this experiment supports the *gesture* layer of the factorisation and does not, on its own, support the *time* layer -- a task that resolves the trajectory shape rather than its endpoint would be needed for that.

  - note: with Phi known, inversion is essentially exact and degrades only as fast as the noise on the transition-averaged trajectory. The temporal inverse problem is easy; everything hard in this pipeline lives in the acoustic map.
- **ORACLE-INPUT -- can DPhi be estimated by probing the latent state?**
  - ground truth: `{"DPhi(u)": "analytic, verified against finite differences to 1e-9"}`
  - estimate: `{"best relative error": 1.4988883933656743e-05, "at (noise, n_repeat)": "(0.01, 100)"}`
  - error: 1.499e-05 (relative Frobenius error)
  - status: **IDENTIFIABLE**
  - control: the saturating map, run through the identical sweep: its optimum sits at delta = 0.03, interior to the swept range
  - note: on the quadratic map the best step is delta = 3, an edge optimum -- and it should be an edge one: central differences truncate at O(delta^2 * third derivative) and a quadratic map has no third derivative, so the estimator is unbiased at every step and error falls as sigma/(delta sqrt(n)). The bias/variance trade-off only becomes visible on the saturating map, which is why both are swept. Reading the monotone curve as a trade-off would have been a comfortable and wrong conclusion.
- **BLIND -- can DPhi be estimated from acoustics alone?**
  - ground truth: `{"column space of DPhi": "2-dimensional inside R^3"}`
  - estimate: `{"noise 0.01": {"subspace error": 0.1457, "naive Frobenius": 1.228}, "noise 0.1": {"subspace error": 0.2024, "naive Frobenius": 1.315}, "noise 0.3": {"subspace error": 0.2375, "naive Frobenius": 1.167}, "noise 1.0": {"subspace error": 0.4952, "naive Frobenius": 1.322}}`
  - error: 0.2024 (sin of the largest principal angle at noise 0.1)
  - status: **PARTIALLY IDENTIFIABLE**
  - control: the same quantity scored in Frobenius norm, which stays near 1 at every noise level
  - note: the tangent *subspace* is recovered (sin theta = 0.202) while the matrix itself is not (Frobenius 1.32). Blind inversion of A->B has direct error 1.19, dropping to 0.052 once an affine reparameterisation of the latent is allowed. That gap IS Hypothesis 2: what is recoverable is the equivalence class, not the coordinates.

  - status: **PARTIALLY IDENTIFIABLE**
  - control: the affine reparameterisation residual, which is the same quantity modulo h
  - note: the two latents differ by 42% and agree to 5.74e-02 once an affine map is allowed. The recoverable object is the equivalence class (Phi, F) / coordinate changes -- Hypothesis 2, measured.
- **What IS invariant -- do the two models share a recoverable quantity?**

  - status: **IDENTIFIABLE**
  - control: the state matrices themselves differ by 9%
  - note: a change of latent basis conjugates the state matrix, and conjugation preserves the spectrum. So the poles are the right thing to report, and A_u is not -- two estimators disagreeing about A_u may be describing exactly the same dynamics.

  - error: 0.4614 (accuracy drop from interpolation to unseen speaker (structured))
  - status: **PARTIALLY IDENTIFIABLE**
  - control: the interpolation split for the same model
  - note: drop for the generic model: 0.236; for the structured model: 0.461. Both are zero-shot -- no adaptation data from the new speaker is given to anyone.
- **ABLATION -- is it the dynamics that carry the structured model?**

  - status: **PARTIALLY IDENTIFIABLE**
