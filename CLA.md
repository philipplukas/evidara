## How to sign

Until a CLA assistant is installed on the repository (see
[CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md#contributor-license-agreement)), sign by **copying the block below
into a comment on your first pull request**:

```text
I have read the Evidara Individual Contributor License Agreement (CLA.md, version 1.0)
and I agree to it for this and all my future Contributions to Evidara.

Full name:
GitHub username:
E-mail:
Date:
```

Your name, GitHub username and e-mail become part of the public record of the pull request. That is
the point — the agreement has to be attributable to be worth anything.

## What was changed, and why

Against the Apache ICLA V2.2, this document:

1. **Substitutes the parties.** "The Apache Software Foundation (the 'Foundation')" becomes "Philipp
   Guldimann (the 'Owner')"; "the Foundation" becomes "the Owner" throughout.
2. **Removes the postal signing instructions and the personal-data form** (full name, public name,
   residence address, country, preferred Apache id, notify-project), replacing them with the
   pull-request comment procedure above. No residential address is collected.
3. **Removes the Foundation's nonprofit undertaking and replaces it with section 4**, which states
   plainly that the Owner may also license the Work commercially. This is the one substantive change
   and the reason a CLA is being used instead of a DCO: a DCO certifies provenance, it does not grant
   the Owner a copyright licence, and without that grant the Owner cannot offer a commercial licence
   covering a contributor's code.
4. **Adds a cross-reference to the Corporate CLA** in section 5, where the ICLA already referred to a
   "separate Corporate CLA".
5. **Reflows the text as Markdown** with numbered headings. Sections 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 8 and 9 are the
   ICLA's sections 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 8 with no changes beyond the party substitution.

The grant language in sections 2 and 3 — the part that actually matters — is the ICLA's, verbatim
apart from the party names. It is the same language the Apache License 2.0 section 5 assumes, so it
is well understood and has been signed by tens of thousands of contributors.
